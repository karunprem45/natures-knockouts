# Paired analysis of the coordination penalty

**Post-freeze extension, not pre-registered.** This comparator was chosen after seeing results, consistent with the multiplicity disclosure in section 7.1 of the report: the secondary contrasts, and now the way the penalty is tested, were picked after the data were in. ## Results

| Statistic | Value |
| --- | --- |
| n (replicates) | 5 |
| Mean | **-0.0533** |
| SD | 0.0294 |
| Standard error | 0.0131 |
| 95% t-interval (df = 4, t = 2.776) | **-0.0897 to -0.0168** |
| Paired t-test, two-sided (H0: mean penalty = 0) | t = -4.06, **p = 0.0154** |
| Paired bootstrap over replicates (10,000 resamples), 95% percentile interval | -0.0753 to -0.0301 |
| Sign test | 5/5 negative, one-sided p = 0.031 |

**Fragility, stated.** With n = 5 the t-interval leans on a normality assumption that five points cannot check, and the bootstrap over five values can only draw from 126 distinct resamples, so its interval is too narrow rather than too wide. The two agree that the interval excludes zero; neither should be read as precise. The sign test makes no distributional assumption at all.

## Why the paired comparator is the right one here

The coordination penalty compares two scorings of the **same** board: the round-0 verdicts and the final verdicts after needs, skeptic and revisions. Run-to-run variation (which genes the model happened to rate higher in a given run) is shared by both sides, so it largely cancels in the difference. The right question is therefore whether the within-run difference is consistently non-zero across runs, which is what the paired t-test, the bootstrap and the sign test ask. Comparing it to the 0.062 floor, which measures how much two **independent** runs disagree, overstates the noise this contrast faces.

The 0.062 floor still applies to **unpaired** contrasts, where the two sides come from different runs: for example round 0 vs A1 (+0.080, 1.3× the floor), the Evo 2 runs vs A3, and the constrained planner vs round 0. Those differences include the full run-to-run variation, so the floor (0.062) is the right yardstick for them.
