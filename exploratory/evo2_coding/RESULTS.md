# T3: Evo 2 on coding variants

**Post-freeze extension, not pre-registered in frozen/.** The prediction was committed before any variant was collected or scored ([`PREDICTION.md`](PREDICTION.md)). Evo 2 ran on NVIDIA's hosted 40B endpoint (`/forward`, `unembed`); Brev was not used. Generated by `exploratory/evo2_coding/analyze.py`.

## The variants

200 predicted loss-of-function variants from Ensembl, 5 per gene for all 40 genes (stop_gained 188, splice_acceptor_variant 5, splice_donor_variant 4, frameshift_variant 3). Scored: 200; reference-base mismatches or other errors: 0.

Median site percentile of the scored substitutions: **4.4** (GWAS lead variants: 59). Share below the 10th percentile: 0.66. Median of per-gene medians: known targets 3.4, negatives 5.2.

## Verdict on the prediction: Not supported

Prediction: the Variant channel contributes more than +0.038. Measured: -0.006. Noise floor (T1, 5 replicates): 0.062; this contrast is 0.1× the floor.

**What this says about the diagnosis.** The coding variants were far more disruptive to Evo 2 than the lead tags (median percentile 4.4 vs 59), so the earlier diagnosis was right at the DNA level. But they were about equally disruptive in known targets (3.4) and in negatives (5.2). Evo 2 measures whether a variant breaks the gene, not whether breaking it is good for the carrier, and only the second separates targets from negatives here. Every panel gene was chosen for having protective loss-of-function burden evidence, so near-universal disruption carries no ranking information. Better variants were necessary but not sufficient.

**Why the channel stays small by design.** The Variant agent only answers needs in round 1; its answers reach the score only through other agents' revisions in round 2, inside the same 10-call budget. So even perfect DNA-level evidence moves AUROC only indirectly. The coding variants do look far more disruptive to Evo 2 than the lead tags did; whether that reaches the ranking is what the table above measures.
